\section{Discussion}\label{a:discussion}
The rate windows are computable, and the set of indices of scalable-memory windows is uncomputable. Interleaving alone rules out a positive noise threshold for these circuits. For a prescribed encoding and recovery, each rational-rate fidelity
test is decidable by an exact finite calculation
(Propositions~\ref{a:rational} and~\ref{v:certification}(ii)). A failed certificate has a finite,
exactly checkable witness \((L,t,q)\). Deciding whether every test in
the certificate passes is undecidable for the circuits of Theorems~\ref{thm:exponential},
\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{q1:main} (Theorem~\ref{a:complete}, Proposition~\ref{v:certification}(ii) and Section~\ref{q1:effective}).
Finite-size experiments still give information at the tested sizes and times. Xu and coauthors obtain a related one-sided certification result for the bulk spectral gap, with convergent certified upper bounds and no general convergent lower bounds \cite{XuEtAl2026}.

Hamiltonian undecidability and undecidable storage both concern observables that depend on arbitrarily large systems. In spectral-gap and phase-diagram results the observable is a property of the infinite-system Hamiltonian or its ground states, and a varying interaction can encode a machine input \cite{BCW2021,CPGW2015}. Here the gates remain fixed and depolarization varies. Its local statistics select the machine input, and the undecidable observable is whether the best recoverable entanglement fidelity supports storage times unbounded in system size. The open windows establish stability under changes of the noise rate within each window.

The constructions use local operations and independent depolarization after every layer, including reset and idle layers. Theorem~\ref{thm:four-dimensional}'s whole-site-noise guarantee is superpolynomial on every
nonhalting window. The small exponent limits the practical significance
of that asymptotic improvement. Stability on an open interval of noise rates leaves open the question of stability under perturbations of the gates.

The forgetting argument of Section~\ref{sec:forgetting} is a tool for other circuits with autonomous classical control. It couples the whole channel to an equal-marginal extension when the chance that a specified input site still matters is small. The cloning bound then limits recovery even when the encoding uses every part of the register.

For the six-dimensional circuits, a matching \(\exp(O_n(L))\) upper bound would give exponential lifetime of order \(\exp(\Theta_n(L))\) on a nonhalting window. The known upper bound uses full resets of individual four-dimensional fibres. The prescribed diagonal defect bands of Lemma~\ref{v:wrapped} erase a plane in the clean comparison rule, but their probability of forming does not give an erasure rate under continuing noise. The four-dimensional whole-site circuit has the full-register bound \(\exp(O_n(L^4))\). A matching stretched-exponential upper bound is open.

Under independent single-qubit noise, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives linear
storage on every nonhalting window in three dimensions. The
four-dimensional circuit of Proposition~\ref{p:coexistence} gives
exponential storage on its reserved window \(I_0\) and linear storage
on its nonhalting tail windows \(I_{n+1}\), which part~(iii) caps at
\(201L+C_0\) rounds. Beyond-polynomial storage on every nonhalting
window remains open for four-dimensional undecidable constructions
under independent single-qubit noise. Theorem~\ref{thm:exponential}
gives an exponential lower bound on every nonhalting window in six
dimensions. Can an undecidable construction under the elementary-layer
noise convention achieve an exponential lower bound on every nonhalting
window below six dimensions, or a superlinear lower bound on every
nonhalting window in three dimensions? Theorem~\ref{q1:main} gives undecidable storage with rate-selected whole-register forgetting already on rings, under independent single-qubit noise. Its linear lower bound leaves open stronger one-dimensional lifetimes and practical reductions of its register size and interaction range. Under whole-site depolarization, undecidable constructions below three dimensions remain open; the rate selection on rings uses independent wire faults within a site. Corollary~\ref{q1:memory} also gives a translation-invariant, time-periodic memory on rings in which every wire, including all control, is noisy, starting from an initialized classical hierarchy. Whether such a hierarchy can be grown from a homogeneous initial state, as in G\'acs's self-organizing automaton \cite{Gacs2001}, remains open here, as it does for the two-dimensional scheme of D\"unnweber, Styliaris and Trivedi \cite{DST2026}. Adapting that two-dimensional automaton or the toric-code circuit of Balasubramanian, Davydova and Lake \cite{BDL2026} to rate-selected storage would require control of the controller-correlated erasures specific to our construction. The homogeneous decoder of Lake and the time-translation-invariant decoder of Selub, Bhardwaj and Lake assume noiseless classical processing \cite{Lake2025,SBL2026}. It is open whether their guarantees extend to the noisy rate-selection and erasure mechanism here. Further questions for undecidable constructions concern smaller site registers, nearest-neighbour interactions and a gate-local noise model with independent replacement of each gate's joint operand register by a maximally mixed state.
