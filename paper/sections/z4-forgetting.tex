\subsection{Halting destroys the entire physical algebra}
\begin{lemma}[Ancestry for the folded schedule]\label{f4:ancestor}
On a halting window, a specified physical initial site has retained
quantum ancestry at depth \(s\) with probability at most
\(A'_n(1+s)^{b'_{\rm c}}e^{-a'_ns}\) in an injectively unwrapped
determining cone. Here \(A'_n,a'_n>0\) and the fixed integer
\(b'_{\rm c}\) are uniform in the window
and simultaneously over every initial metadata word. All quantum
factors at that site are included in the same event.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The cap's negative argument is the one in Lemma~\ref{v:ancestor}.
We verify its graph hypotheses and the changed quantum path explicitly.
The static alternative there uses original profiles, active-neighbor
death ancestry and the deep-only base dictionary. Deleting copied
coordinates changes none of them. On halting, every arbitrary active
root is within bounded \(n\)-dependent original-active path distance
of a local violation, a fully guarded wrong critical motif, or a valid
uniformly bounded square. Finite-alphabet compactness covers malformed,
originless and wrapped components, exactly as in that proof. Persistent
or dead profiles remain distinct from vacuum, so cap boundaries and
timer endpoints are unchanged.

Full-core finite-age coherence and the all-path density bound of
Lemma~\ref{v:density} identify one immutable profile in a flag-only
good cone. The explicit active-neighbor death terms propagate ancestry
to the obstruction. A violation or arbitrary-phase expiry of a bounded
square contradicts late permission. At a wrong motif a genuine origin
flushes initial helper garbage. Historical bottom writes unknown/allowed
and cannot conceal either wrong critical test. A surviving sample pays
an intrinsic/overlay packet or a harmful nonbottom deep profile error.
The depth-4096 argument in Section~\ref{v:parameters} gives at least
sixteen distinct actual raw names per harmful query. Full-support slab
packing retains the last complete contour, including its part beyond
the sample prefix. Temporal coverage is
\(\lfloor t/K_{\rm age}\rfloor\le J_{\rm pack}(n)(f+m_{\rm pkt})\),
with \(J_{\rm pack}(n)=C_{\rm deep}+C_{\rm end}+8w_n+1\).
The same pre-helper historical entropy counts all possible profiles,
without conditioning on any initial word or chosen provider path.
H\"older's inequality between possibly overlapping families gives
\(p_H^{3f/4}\theta_{\rm pkt}^{m_{\rm pkt}/4}\), and
\[
 (2\mu)^{3f/4}(C_h/\mu)^{6f/16}
       =(2C_h)^{3f/8}(2\mu)^{3f/8}.
\]
The first two strict negative inequalities make the sum exponentially
small. Polynomial cone unions yield the all-start permission/proposal
tail, uniformly in the window. Added calendar metadata is autonomous
and contributes only to already counted internal overlays.

For center ancestry at fixed \((x,y)\), use the minimal fields
\[
 \mathcal B_t=H_t\lor H_{t+1},\quad
 \mathcal W_t=\mathcal B_t\lor\Theta_{512}(\mathcal W_{t-1}),\quad
 \mathcal N_t=\Theta_{512}(\mathcal W_t),
\]
started empty. Late permission outside that field would give a center
path to the initial row; its late half is raw-marked by the preceding
tail, contrary to all-path density except for an exponential event.
A new \(\mathcal N\) birth needs 256 distinct fresh providers or old
support in two boxes. The same two-dimensional quorum plane
\((\sigma,z)\) has four spatial parities and two time parities on
full doubled cubes of side 352. As in Lemma~\ref{v:ancestor}, for
\(g\) seed groups and \(k_{\rm dir}\) distinct direct names the joint weight is
\[
 (N_{\rm seed}p_H^{256})^{g/16}p_H^{k_{\rm dir}/2}.
\]
Full supports are reserved at every restart; complete rare contours
are attached to the actual quantum path.

For this schedule quantum edges move only a bounded distance in
\(\sigma\) or between finitely many onsite role/port/work factors.
They keep \(x,y,z\) fixed. All spatial quantum work precedes the
complete own-site permission cut. A forbidden clean cut removes every
incoming quantum dependence; reset/preparation has no incoming edge,
and a discarded factor never returns. Thus every surviving path is a
connected description in the finite negative catalogue used to define
\(K_{\rm rare},B_{\rm rare}\). It has
\(g+k_{\rm dir}\ge s/B_{\rm rare}\) and at most
\(K_{\rm rare}^{g+k_{\rm dir}}\) descriptions. The last two negative inequalities
sum this tail exponentially. A polynomial determining-cone union proves
the lemma for a specified initial physical site, including both fold
roles and all work.

Every assertion averages the actual independent local whole-site
flags and their conditional product helper marks. Arbitrary joint
quantum coefficients are an analysis of that physical circuit. Fresh
replacement/reset environments are never retrieved, so they supply
no returning path. Arbitrary initial programs, endpoints, timers and
calendar words are included; calendar synchronization is unnecessary
for this negative argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{f4:detailed}(ii)]
Initially dephase every autonomous metadata register. At each gate it
is either involved in metadata-only Boolean/reset/dephasing work or
is a control; a quantum-derived register never targets it. Given the
initial classical word and a complete innovation history, the quantum
evolution is CPTP with a fixed dependence graph. Every quantum lifetime
crosses its complete cut or ends at reset/disposal. Hence
Lemma~\ref{f:factorization} applies to one physical initial-site mask,
retaining all its quantum factors if any has an output descendant.
The initially dephased algebra, including its reference correlations,
is retained and broadcast in the channel extension.

For a fixed sufficiently large \(s\), partition a large four-torus
into blocks of sides between \(s\) and \(2s\).
Lemma~\ref{f4:ancestor} and a union over \(O(s^4)\) sites make the
block-ancestor marginals small. Their determining radius is \(O(s)\),
so the finite dependency degree is fixed. Lemma~\ref{f:domination}
gives fair-mask domination. Proposition~\ref{f:extension} then uses
complementary disjoint physical masks and two complete conditional
histories to reproduce the same entire channel in each output.
This keeps all cap/calendar/hardware correlations, all initial
intersite entanglement and the full metadata algebra.
Lemmas~\ref{f:iteration}--\ref{f:bell} give \(2^j\) outputs at a
finite time, with optimal fidelity at most \(1/2+2^{-j-1}\).
Choose \(j\) for \(\eta\); the size condition is the one for fixed
\(s\).

Finitely many smaller sizes remain. Since \(I_n=(a_n,b_n)\) has
\(a_n>0\), complete whole-register replacement in an elementary layer
has probability at least \(a_n^{L^4}\), uniformly in the window.
Independent repetitions give
\(F^*\le1/4+(3/4)(1-a_n^{L^4})^t\).
Take the maximum of the finite required times and the large-torus time,
converting rounds to elementary layers. Later channels are absorbed
into the decoder. The result is the finite \(C_{n,\eta}\) uniform
in every size, rate, encoder and decoder claimed above.
\end{proof}

For each fixed rational \(0<\eta<3/8\), no total computable bound
on these halting times as a function of unpromised \(n\) exists.
Indeed such a bound would let one choose computably a rational rate
inside \(I_n\), a time beyond the bound, and \(L\ge L_4(n)\) with
that time below \(\exp(c_4L^{\alpha_4})\); computing the fidelity of
the explicit finite encoder/decoder to an accuracy smaller than the
gap between \(1/2+\eta\) and \(15/16\) would decide halting.
The finite channel entries and these external circuits are computable,
as in Proposition~\ref{a:rational}.
