\section{Structural control and dependent witnesses}\label{q1:clock}

Write \(V^j=[0,P^j)\times[0,T^j)\) on the site--date lattice. A set is 0-sparse if it is empty; it is \(j\)-sparse if every translate \(Q\) of \(wV^j\) admits a translate \(J\) of \(3wV^{j-1}\) such that \((E\cap Q)\setminus J\) is \((j-1)\)-sparse. Every restricted set is extended by zero outside its restriction. Subsets inherit sparsity. These are the classical sparsity conventions throughout the following proofs.

\begin{lemma}[restricted witness trees and their probability]\label{q1:l1}

Let \(P_{R},T_{R},w_{R}\) be the fixed structural \(R1\) geometry. Let \(E_{R}\) be a native flag field on \(\Z\times\Z\) whose flag at \(q\) is the indicator that at least one independent elementary depolarization flag in a finite stencil \(\Sigma_{R}(q)\) is one. Assume every stencil has at most \(s_{R} \ge  1\) keys and is contained, in site/macrodate coordinates, within radii \(r_{x},r_{t}\) of \(q\). Negative-time native flags are set to zero; stencils at nonnegative times use only physical keys at nonnegative elementary times. Rates of all these keys are at most \(e\), allowing one elementary-rate splice.

Set
\[
\begin{gathered}
D_{R} = 8(P_{R}+2)^{2}(T_{R}+2)^{2}, \\ N_{R} = 4(P_{R}+3)(T_{R}+3), \\ A_{R} = 4w_{R}^{2} s_{R}, \\ J_{R} = (2w_{R}+4r_{x}+3)(2w_{R}+4r_{t}+3), \\ \Delta_{R} = J_{R}-1,  c_{R} = 1/J_{R}, \\ C_{R} = N_{R} D_{R} A_{R}^{1/J_{R}}, \\ e_{R0} = A_{R}^{-1}(2N_{R} D_{R})^{-J_{R}}.
\end{gathered}
\]

For any prescribed protected rank-\(j\) \(W_0\) test of width \(<w_{R} P_{R}^{j}\) and height \(<w_{R} T_{R}^{j}\), its failure of \((j-1)\)-sparsity has probability at most
\[
\begin{gathered}
z_{R,j}(e) = [C_{R} e^{c_{R}}]^{2^{j-1}},  j \ge  1.
\end{gathered}
\]
In particular \(C_{R} e^{c_{R}} \le  1/2\) for \(0 \le  e \le  e_{R0}\). These are explicit computable constants for the final fixed circuit, not asymptotic constants whose choice can change with \(j\).

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use sparsity as in \cite[Definition~\ref*{cl:def:sparse}]{DMNoise1D2026}, with restricted sets extended by zero. For an arbitrary deterministic flag set \(S\), the mesh argument in \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:sparse-witness}]{DMNoise1D2026} says the following: nonsparsity at rank \(k+1\) inside one doubled rank-\((k+1)\) box implies rank-\(k\) nonsparsity in two disjoint doubled rank-\(k\) candidate boxes, with at most \(D_{R}\) choices of their unordered pair. The set used for either child is \(S\) restricted to the parent. This implication is deterministic and does not use independence. We keep this restriction at every step. It makes the argument below independent of how the candidate boxes are placed: with the flush placement of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:sparse-witness}]{DMNoise1D2026} they lie inside the parent, while a placement with one exterior mesh cell can put the lower mesh corner used to enclose a moving window one mesh cell outside the parent.

Iterate this implication \(k\) times, retaining the restriction to every ancestor at each node. A leaf has a region \(R\) equal to its doubled rank-zero box intersected with all its ancestor boxes (and the initial restricted root). It is nonempty and contains a flag, because 0-sparsity means emptiness. It has at most \(4w_{R}^{2}\) lattice sites. There are \(N=2^{k}\) leaves. Their regions are pairwise disjoint: two distinct leaves separate into the disjoint sibling boxes of their last common ancestor, and all subsequent restrictions remain in those boxes. The argument uses only the disjointness of these regions, not that of the leaf boxes themselves, which can fail for a placement with exterior mesh cells.

For a prescribed tree, join two leaves if the unions of primitive-key stencils over their regions intersect. If \(R\) has an adjacent leaf \(R'\), some \(q\) in \(R\) and \(q'\) in \(R'\) obey \(|q_{x}-q'_{x}| \le  2r_{x}\) and \(|q_{t}-q'_{t}| \le  2r_{t}\). The doubled rank-zero box containing \(R\) has sides at most \(2w_{R}\). Each adjacent region supplies a different lattice point in the rectangle enlarged by \(2r_{x},2r_{t}\), because the regions are disjoint. The area bound is less than \(J_{R}\). Thus this conflict graph has degree at most \(\Delta_{R}\). A fixed lexicographic greedy algorithm selects at least \(N/J_{R}\) leaves with disjoint primitive-key sets. Each selected leaf requires at least one of at most \(A_{R}\) elementary flags, so its probability is at most \(A_{R} e\) by a union bound. Their events are independent, even though the original native flags are dependent. For \(A_{R} e \le  1\) their joint probability is at most \((A_{R} e)^{N/J_{R}}\).

There are at most \(D_{R}^{N-1}\) geometric trees from a fixed root doubled box: the full binary depth-\(k\) topology is fixed and each internal node has at most \(D_{R}\) pair choices. No extra choices of flagged sites are necessary; the leaf probability already sums those choices.

If the rank-\(k\) test fails on a \(W_0\) of size \(<w_{R} P_{R}^{k+1}\) by \(w_{R} T_{R}^{k+1}\), a failing moving rank-\(k\) window meeting its restricted flag set fits in one doubled rank-\(k\) box from the \(w_{R} P_{R}^{k}\) by \(w_{R} T_{R}^{k}\) mesh. There are at most \(N_{R}\) such boxes, even allowing an exterior row and column of the mesh. For \(k=0\) the same cover simply locates a nonempty leaf region. Union over these roots and their trees gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{Pr}(\mathrm{failure}) \le  N_{R} D_{R}^{N-1} (A_{R} e)^{N/J_{R}} \\ \le  [N_{R} D_{R} A_{R}^{1/J_{R}} e^{1/J_{R}}]^{N}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Set \(k=j-1\). The asserted threshold is a direct substitution and also ensures \(A_{R} e \le  1\). Deleting negative-time keys can only decrease these increasing events. Independent unequal key rates bounded above by \(e\) satisfy the same product estimate. This proves all assertions. 
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[effective radius-moment cutoff]\label{q1:l1.1}

Suppose Lemma \ref{q1:l3} gives at most \(N_{\mathrm{clk}}\) protected tests of each rank per query and their entire key supports lie in radius
\[
\begin{gathered}
r_{R,j} \le  C_{\mathrm{clk}} B_{R}^{j},  B_{R} = \max (P_{R},T_{R}),
\end{gathered}
\]
where \(N_{\mathrm{clk}},C_{\mathrm{clk}}\) are positive integers fixed after compilation. For every finite real \(d \ge  0\) and every rational \(\delta > 0\) there is an explicit computable rational \(e_{R}(d,\delta)>0\) such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
\sum _{j\ge 1} N_{\mathrm{clk}} (1+r_{R,j})^{d} \sqrt{z_{R,j}(e)} \le  \delta
\end{gathered}
\]
for every \(0 \le  e \le  e_{R}(d,\delta)\).

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replace \(d\) by its integer ceiling \(d'\). Choose an integer \(j_{*} \ge  1\) satisfying \(2^{j_{*}-1} \ge  d' \lceil \log _{2} B_{R}\rceil+1\). Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
t_{j} = B_{R}^{d' j} 2^{-(2^{j-1}-1)}, \\ S_{\mathrm{bar}} = \sum _{j=1}^{j_{*}-1} t_{j} + 2t_{j_{*}}, \\ K_{R} = N_{\mathrm{clk}}(C_{\mathrm{clk}}+1)^{d'} S_{\mathrm{bar}}, \\ q_{R} = \min (1/4, (\delta/K_{R})^{2}), \\ e_{R}(d,\delta) = \min (e_{R0}, A_{R}^{-1}[q_{R}/(N_{R} D_{R})]^{J_{R}}).
\end{gathered}
\]
All these quantities are rational and effective. If \(x=C_{R} e^{1/J_{R}}\), this rate bound ensures \(x\le q_{R}\le 1/4\). Then
\[
\begin{gathered}
x^{2^{j-2}} \le  \sqrt{x} 2^{-(2^{j-1}-1)}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Also \((1+C_{\mathrm{clk}} B_{R}^{j})^{d} \le  (C_{\mathrm{clk}}+1)^{d'} B_{R}^{d'j}\). The ratio \(t_{j+1}/t_{j}\) equals \(B_{R}^{d'} 2^{-2^{j-1}}\) and is at most \(1/2\) for \(j\ge j_{*}\). Hence \(\sum  t_{j} \le  S_{\mathrm{bar}}\). The desired moment is at most \(K_{R} \sqrt{x} \le  \delta\). This establishes convergence, vanishing as \(e\) tends to zero, and an effective uniform cutoff, rather than only a dominated-convergence argument. 
\end{proof}

\textbf{Order of choice.} The stencil constants refer to the final fixed physical circuit, including all idle layers. They are chosen before the final physical rate-window cutoff. In the assembly, fix the native helper \(n_{\mathrm{bin}}\) before that circuit and use the last-chosen arithmetic padding \(J_{\mathrm{pad}}\) to skip native bins; do not enlarge \(n_{\mathrm{bin}}\) after estimating \(s_{R}\). Thus the dependence of \(s_{R}\) on the elementary period length creates no circular choice. \(C_{R}, J_{R}, N_{R}\) and the rate cutoff are the explicit constants of the probability estimate in Lemma~\ref{q1:l1}.

\begin{lemma}[packing at an admission rank]\label{q1:l1.2}

The same restricted-witness construction can be stopped at any rank \(l\). Suppose the event assigned to a rank-\(l\) leaf has primitive support contained in that leaf's full box enlarged by \(a_{x} w_{R} P_{R}^{l}\) in space and \(a_{t} w_{R} T_{R}^{l}\) in time, with fixed \(a_{x},a_{t} \ge  0\). Then its conflict graph has degree at most
\[
\begin{gathered}
J_{\mathrm{adm}}-1,  J_{\mathrm{adm}} = \lceil 8+4a_{x}\rceil \lceil 8+4a_{t}\rceil,
\end{gathered}
\]
uniformly in \(l\) and in the depth of the tree. Initial clipping by an arbitrary \(W_0\) does not invalidate this bound. Thus at least the fraction \(1/J_{\mathrm{adm}}\) of the leaves have disjoint primitive supports.

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the root box and construct every descendant mesh relative to its lower corner. Because \(P_{R},T_{R}\) are integers, boundaries of all boxes at ranks \(\ge l\) lie on the same translated mesh with cell sides \(\ell_{x}=w_{R} P_{R}^{l}, \ell_{t}=w_{R} T_{R}^{l}\). For a leaf \(L\), use the ancestor region \(R_{\mathrm{anc}}(L)\), the intersection of its full box with its ancestor boxes, without imposing the initial \(W_0\) restriction. These ancestor regions are disjoint by the sibling-separation argument in the proof of Lemma \ref{q1:l1}. An event witnessed on the actual clipped region implies \(R_{\mathrm{anc}}\) is nonempty. It is a rectangle whose boundary coordinates are on the common mesh, so it contains a whole \(\ell_{x}\) by \(\ell_{t}\) mesh cell. Choose one such cell deterministically.

If the enlarged support of another leaf meets that of \(L\), its full box has its lower corner within \((2+2a_{x})\ell_{x},(2+2a_{t})\ell_{t}\) of the lower corner of \(L\). Its selected mesh cell is therefore inside a rectangle with respective side lengths at most \((6+4a_{x})\ell_{x},(6+4a_{t})\ell_{t}\) around that corner. Distinct leaves have distinct selected cells, since their ancestor regions are disjoint. The number of available cells, with two extra boundary cells in each coordinate allowed, is at most \(J_{\mathrm{adm}}\). This bounds all conflicts, including multiplicity from geometrically coincident full boxes in different ancestor branches. The proof did not pack the possibly thin \(W_0\)-clipped regions. Greedy selection gives the final assertion. 
\end{proof}

This lemma supplies the bounded-overlap step for the two-scale admission proof when the primitive support is shorter than a leaf side. It does not assume that the globally untruncated leaf boxes are disjoint or that clipped regions have a rank-uniform positive area.



\begin{lemma}[total classical interface for \(R1\)]\label{q1:l2}

Fix the final physical elementary circuit of Section~\ref{q1:model} and the \(R1\) packet serialization of Lemma~\ref{q1:l9}. Let \(\mathrm{Alph}_{R}\) be the finite native \(R1\) alphabet at physical rank zero. Fix an injective canonical binary representation \(\operatorname{enc}_{R}\):\(\mathrm{Alph}_{R} \to  \{0,1\}^{b_{R}}\). Extend its inverse to a total parser \(\operatorname{dec}_{R}\) by mapping every unused binary string to the dead native symbol \(D\). The \(R1\) Boolean evaluator computes \(\operatorname{enc}_{R}(f_{R}(\operatorname{dec}_{R}(w_{-}),\operatorname{dec}_{R}(w_{0}),\operatorname{dec}_{R}(w_{+})))\) on arbitrary incoming banks; any additional unused Boolean intermediate strings have fixed total defaults. This only specifies the already required finite Boolean compilation on malformed inputs.

There is a finite stencil \(\Sigma_{R}(x,t)\) with the following properties. The decoded-symbol implication of Lemma \ref{q1:l3} uses only properties (i) and (iv) below; property (ii) is proved afterwards, using that implication.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The decoded positive-time history is a native \(R1\) data history off the flag set \(E_{R}(x,t)=\one[\text{at least one primitive flag in }\Sigma_{R}(x,t)]\). Corruption of a legal word and production of a non-codeword both fall within this implication.
\item At every actual quantum-processor read, all raw age, address, retained-word low bits and required tag bits agree with their canonical clean values unless a protected clock test fails at some rank. This covers a physical raw read, not just the value of \(\operatorname{dec}_{R}\).
\item Conditional on the dephased initial \(R1\) row, the \(R1\) marginal is a classical chain depending only on its own independent primitive innovations. The local terminal erase never erases or writes into the retained autonomous \(R1\) banks. All private copies used as quantum controls belong to disposable or cut quantum work.
\item Concatenating the clean negative tower with the actual initialized history at date zero gives a native data history with no negative-time flags.
\end{enumerate}

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A dephased finite-alphabet transition is a finite Boolean truth table. Its total truth table includes the parser/default convention just stated; canonical inputs produce the prescribed canonical output. Implement it on private initialized targets, with old and new banks separate, and use private destination copies for simultaneous stencil readers, as prescribed in Section~\ref{q1:model}. In a realization with no physical noise on the complete implementing stencil, deterministic evaluation is exactly that truth table, irrespective of what incoming legal or malformed strings were present. Every disagreement of the output decoder with the native transition is consequently contained in the event that this stencil has a flag.

The complete stencil includes: each incoming bank's storage from its preceding completed write through the final relevant read; all one-way fanout copies and their storage; every elementary gate of the total parser and Boolean evaluator; all old/new bank transfers; every reset, the mandatory noise immediately after it, and each private target's storage before it is read. It also includes the raw source bits through each actual quantum-control read, including a directly copied raw low bit. There are only finitely many elementary layers and fixed spatial displacements in a physical period. Hence its size and its coordinate radii can be found by finite enumeration, giving \(s_{R},r_{x},r_{t}\) in Lemma \ref{q1:l1}. To remove an indexing ambiguity, \(\Sigma_{R}(x,t)\) is defined to include both the incoming physical round \(t-1\) and the outgoing round \(t\): this ensures that both the transition into native date \(t\) and every control read at date \(t\) are covered. At \(t=0\) retain only nonnegative elementary-time keys. Overlap between successive stencils is allowed and is precisely why Lemma \ref{q1:l1} uses a conflict graph rather than iid native flags.

Off \(\Sigma_{R}(x,t)\), the word delivered at the native boundary is canonical. If its parsed symbol differs from the clean native symbol, Lemma \ref{q1:l3}'s projection implication applies. If instead a delivered raw bank or read copy is noncanonical, or differs in a raw bit while its parsed symbol happens to agree, then some key of \(\Sigma_{R}(x,t)\) (evaluator, output write, storage or read copy) is flagged. The prescribed rank-one \(W_0\) containing \((x,t)\), including its seed and two work periods, contains this native flag point. Its required 0-sparsity therefore fails. If a different canonical valid symbol was delivered, its raw differing bits are already covered by the decoded-symbol case. These alternatives exhaust all raw read words. The rank-one raw-read test is one of the bounded fixed-offset tests used in Lemma \ref{q1:l3}; if necessary retain its identical translate explicitly, rather than assuming a parser tells physical gates how to act.

For closure, at a round boundary the retained autonomous banks are dephased. Their joint state with all other registers has a classical-quantum form \(\sum _{a} \lvert a\rangle\langle a\rvert\otimes\rho_{a}\). Autonomous updates and copies between autonomous banks are basis permutations or total classical channels built from the truth table. An autonomous bank is only a source of a one-way control into a private target; there is no quantum-target-to-autonomous write. For an arbitrary controlled target unitary, even with arbitrary correlations in \(\rho_{a}\), tracing out the targets leaves the probabilities of a unchanged. The same holds for a private copy followed by discard. A depolarization on a basis-valued autonomous bit is an independent classical replacement by a fair bit. Induction over elementary layers proves that the autonomous marginal is the stated classical chain. Private quantum controls can fault, but their faults are included in the read/dispatch stencil or in the direct hardware mark, and cannot inject an unflagged quantum result into that marginal.

The \(D\)-triggered erase acts on every retained quantum-capable factor, as specified in Section~\ref{q1:forgetting} but not on the autonomous dephased banks. An \(R1\) schedule tape inside a quantum gadget is a different factor: it remains quantum-capable and is erased. This distinction is by named factors and one-way gate incidence, not by an intended classical interpretation. It establishes the claimed exclusion of the real autonomous \(R1\) lane.

Finally the encoder supplies \(\operatorname{enc}_{R}\) of the exact time-zero boundary constructed in the proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:past}]{DMNoise1D2026}. Use the clean exact tower at all negative dates and the actual decoded trajectory at nonnegative dates. There is no discontinuity of the row at the join. Negative updates are exact, and positive decoded-update failures have the stencil certificates just proved. Define \(E_{R}=0\) at negative native dates; no fictitious negative physical innovation is needed for this lane. This is a data history of precisely the type quantified in \cite[Proposition~\ref*{cl:prop:organization}]{DMNoise1D2026}. 
\end{proof}



\begin{lemma}[initialized structural projection on the line]\label{q1:l3}

Choose once and for all a certified never-halting input to the \(R1\) family, for example the index of a machine whose transition graph has no halting state. Use its homogeneous true family literal. Work on \(\Z\), not on a globally initialized finite ring. Let the clean negative tower be the exact all-live tower constructed in the proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:past}]{DMNoise1D2026}, at its aligned boundary zero, and use that boundary to initialize the actual compiled lane. Compare the clean tower for \(t<0\) and the actual trajectory for \(t\ge 0\), glued at the identical row zero, as in Lemma \ref{q1:l2}.

For each physical native point \((x,t)\) with \(t\ge 0\), failure of its full native symbol or any of its actual control-read bits to equal the clean trajectory implies failure of one of a deterministic collection of protected \(W_0\) tests. At rank \(j\) there are at most \(N_{\mathrm{clk}}\) tests. Each requires \((j-1)\)-sparsity and has its entire key support within radii \(C_{\mathrm{clk}} P_{R}^{j},C_{\mathrm{clk}} T_{R}^{j}\) of the query, for fixed effective integers \(N_{\mathrm{clk}},C_{\mathrm{clk}}\). The symbol includes every mutable native field, scratch and the retained letter, including a letter that encodes another retained letter.

One explicit conservative choice is
\[
\begin{gathered}
W_{*} = \lceil (2T_{R}+18P_{R})/(P_{R}-1)\rceil + 2, \\ N_{\mathrm{clk}} = W_{*} + 8, \\ C_{\mathrm{det}} = 100(w_{R}+W_{*}+T_{R}+P_{R}+1), \\ C_{\mathrm{clk}} = C_{\mathrm{det}}+r_{x}+r_{t}+2.
\end{gathered}
\]
The extra constant number of rank-one raw-read tests in Lemma \ref{q1:l2} is included in \(N_{\mathrm{clk}}\).

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At rank \(k\) consider a contiguous interval \(J\) of served-symbol queries at a common rank-\(k\) date \(s\). A negative \(s\) uses only the fixed clean past: the total service lookahead is \(<T_{R}^{k}\) physical ticks by the lookahead bound \(b_k\le T_{R}^k-1\) following \cite[Definition~\ref*{cl:def:observations}]{DMNoise1D2026} (also step~4 of the proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:two-period}]{DMNoise1D2026}), so the observation of any rank-\(k\) point with \(s\le -1\) ends at a strictly negative physical date. These are the terminal queries of the recursion.

For \(s\ge 0\), set \(u=\lfloor s/T_{R}\rfloor\) and \(h=u-1\). Enlarge \(J\) by \(T_{R}+2\) rank-\(k\) sites at either end. Take all rank-\((k+1)\) homes meeting the enlarged interval and five more homes on each side. Query their rank-\((k+1)\) served symbols at the single date \(h\). This set supplies every sextuple required below. For each required central home, choose the protected rank-\((k+1)\) \(W_0\) of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:two-period}]{DMNoise1D2026} with that home in its central pair and its first work period starting at \(h\). Require \(k\)-sparsity on the entire \(W_0\). This is exactly the empty-top-exception variant in \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:two-period}]{DMNoise1D2026}; lower-rank sparse defects are permitted, rather than requiring a large raw-empty rectangle.

Suppose the parent queries equal clean. Their sextuples are positive and in the clean common tribe, and the two prescribed upper outputs are positive. Steps 1--3 of the proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:two-period}]{DMNoise1D2026} apply. The first period starts at \(hT_{R}\) and ends at \((h+1)T_{R}=uT_{R}\). At that last ballot both central child homes have the exact retained packet at every position, correct ordinary addresses, zero age and all scratch defaults. This is not merely nominal acceptance. The actual row is given by step 3 of that proof, with the ballot rule of \cite[Section~\ref*{cl:sec:verifier}]{DMNoise1D2026} and the full-home specialization of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:endpoint}]{DMNoise1D2026}; the clean identity \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:clean}]{DMNoise1D2026} identifies the clean row as \(E_{j}\) with all scratch defaults; they agree field by field. Nominal acceptance can tolerate a common interval of up to \(2q\) bad positions at the first ballot and cannot be used as an exact bottom row.

For completeness the full-state point does not silently omit \(\mathsf{Cn}\) or \(\mathsf{Temp}\), listed separately in \cite[Section~\ref*{cl:sec:native}]{DMNoise1D2026}. The ballot clears scratch and returns the proper ordinary state (\cite[Section~\ref*{cl:sec:verifier}]{DMNoise1D2026} and the full-home specialization of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:endpoint}]{DMNoise1D2026}). Within a full proper home every Purge starts \(\mathsf{Cn}=1\), after which its grade-dependent evolution is uniquely determined by the ordinary address run, including the grade-one wrap. By step 3 of the proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:two-period}]{DMNoise1D2026}, during the first period the left neighbour of address zero of each central home lies in a full normalized home, so its grade is \(1 < 2\); after \(i\) Purge propagation ticks \(\mathsf{Cn}=[\mathrm{address} \ge i]\), as in the clean row. \(\mathsf{Temp}\) is reset every Main cycle (\cite[Section~\ref*{cl:sec:clock}]{DMNoise1D2026}) and no birth can replace an occupied incumbent. Thus any such scalar not included in the literal scratch clearing agrees with its clean value as well. \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:clean}]{DMNoise1D2026} and its proof obtain the complete aligned blocks \(E_j\), with every scratch register reset. This is why the starting row is a full symbol row.

Step 4 of the proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:two-period}]{DMNoise1D2026} now keeps both central homes full and properly served at every date \(uT_{R},\dots,(u+1)T_{R}-1\), including both endpoints in space. At each such protected lower site, \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:endpoint}]{DMNoise1D2026} and the protected exact computation of \cite[Proposition~\ref*{cl:prop:organization}]{DMNoise1D2026} say that its next served full symbol is exactly the native \(F\) on its three actual served inputs. The backward radius-one cone from \(J\) at \(s\) to \(uT_{R}\) has fewer than \(T_{R}\) native steps. Every home it meets was chosen as a central home of its own \(W_0\); the enlargement \(T_{R}+2\) supplies the triple/endpoint reserve. Induction from the exact bottom row at \(uT_{R}\) therefore gives the exact clean state on the requested interval \(J\) at date \(s\). The true family literal is spatially homogeneous, so all immutable operands in that induction are the clean ones. At rank zero the same assertion is just the unflagged physical native update. Served observations are used to prove this implication only; they are not future information fed to a physical gate.

Recurse on the queried parent interval and date \(h\). The interval widths satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
W_{\mathrm{next}} \le  W/P_{R} + 2T_{R}/P_{R} + 18.
\end{gathered}
\]
Indeed the enlargement costs \(2T_{R}+4\) child sites, rounding to homes costs at most two homes and the extra neighbors cost ten; the displayed allowance dominates \(4/P_{R}+12\). The chosen \(W_{*}\) is an invariant upper bound starting from a single-site query. Hence the number of selected parent homes, and therefore \(W_0\) tests at every rank, is at most the asserted \(N_{\mathrm{clk}}\). Dates obey \(s_{\mathrm{next}}=\lfloor s/T_{R}\rfloor-1\), which becomes negative after finitely many iterations for every fixed \(s\).

The same recursion gives explicit finite radii. A lower-to-parent spatial displacement, expressed back in child units, is at most \(T_{R}+7P_{R}+W_{*}\). Summing its physical displacements over \(l=0,\dots,j-1\) gives at most \((T_{R}+7P_{R}+W_{*}) \sum _{l} P_{R}^{l}\). Temporal rounding and the subtraction of one cost \(<2T_{R}^{l+1}\) per promotion; the sum is \(<4T_{R}^{j}\). A \(W_0\) adds \(<w_{R} P_{R}^{j}\) and \(<w_{R} T_{R}^{j}\), together with only its fixed six-input/two-period offsets. For \(P_{R},T_{R}\ge 2\) these are dominated by \(C_{\mathrm{det}} P_{R}^{j},C_{\mathrm{det}} T_{R}^{j}\) with the displayed loose \(C_{\mathrm{det}}\). Finally enlarge by \(r_{x},r_{t}\) for the complete primitive stencils. This gives \(C_{\mathrm{clk}}\).

The construction of the test family is deterministic: select intervals, integer dates and aligned homes before inspecting the raw flags. If every test succeeds, the preceding implication inducts down from the negative terminal queries to the original query. Its contrapositive proves the decoded-symbol statement. Lemma \ref{q1:l2} proves the raw-read extension and adds only the already allowed fixed number of rank-one tests. 
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[effective boundary and precise finite-ring use]\label{q1:l3.1}

Every finite restriction of the line's time-zero row is computable. On a finite ring, Lemma \ref{q1:l3} may be invoked only for physical outputs whose entire actual past dependency cone lies in an injectively embedded interval initialized with that line row. There is no assertion of a clean full-ring \(R1\) tower or of correctness near arbitrary filler.

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The proof of \cite[Lemma~\ref*{cl:lem:past}]{DMNoise1D2026} obtains the boundary independently of the arbitrary upper history: for an integer coordinate \(i\), repeated \(\lfloor i/P_{R}\rfloor\) reaches 0 or -1, after which the next serialized digit is a true endpoint with its complete cell fixed independently of the upper live state. Recursively substitute that endpoint back down. This algorithm terminates for every coordinate and therefore for every finite interval. Compactness supplies a compatible clean negative tower for the proof, but no encoder computes or samples its arbitrary negative-time history.

For the finite ring, initialize this computable row on the interval in Section~\ref{q1:storage} and couple its elementary noise keys to a single infinite-line extension. Induct on actual elementary dependency edges. Gates and initial factors in the real past cone coincide, so the ring and line outputs coincide exactly there. For the \(R1\) marginal, one native update has radius one; its macro-time cone has radius at most the number of elapsed native ticks. Any fixed compilation displacements and partial-period read cones are included in the assembly's \(C_{\mathrm{cone}}\). Thus the interval reserved there suffices.

Lemma \ref{q1:l3}'s auxiliary high-rank certificates may extend beyond the injective interval; they are events in the analysis line extension used to bound the already coupled output. They are not extra ring randomness and no two lifts of a ring key are declared independent. Fillers, seams and nonmultiples of \(P_{R}\) therefore cause no application outside the lemma's line scope. 
\end{proof}
