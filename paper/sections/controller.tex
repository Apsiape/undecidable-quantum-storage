\section{A classical computation that controls erasure}\label{sec:controller}

The classical processor decides when to reset local quantum data. Its noise samples select a computation. We state the imported classical interface, then prove the estimates that connect it to quantum storage and to loss of quantum dependence.

We call the rate-selecting controller the selector. It updates a space-time diagram on a hierarchical tiling. The noise tests can set a symbol to \(\bot\), which spreads under the fault-free update.

\subsection{The selector and its computational chart}\label{c:chart}
For the three-dimensional construction use coordinates
\({\bf z}=(i,P_1,P_2)=(i,\sigma,z_3)\in\Z^3\).
Here the two components of \(P\) are the transverse voting coordinates.
The selector \(C_{\rm sel}\) has chart coordinates
\[
 (x,y)=(i,t-i)
\]
at round \(t\).
In Section~\ref{sec:exponential}, \(P\) has four components. All carry the code, and \(P_1,P_2\) are the voting coordinates. The four-dimensional construction of Appendix~\ref{sec:fold-controls} adds a copied coordinate \(Q\) and four signed orientations. Formulas involving \(Q\) omit that coordinate in three dimensions.
\par\emph{The chart.} A chart alphabet \(\mathcal A\) is extended by a symbol \(\bot\); a chart letter is live when it differs from \(\bot\). The west and south chart neighbours are the actual previous-time sites \((i-1,t-1)\) and \((i,t-1)\), respectively. A partial completion map \(\operatorname{cmp}\) sends a west letter and a south letter to a new letter. It is functional, so there is at most one completion, and bottom-spreading, so a live completion requires two live inputs. The chart history has fixed depth \(T_{\rm hist}=1024\), and its local history consistency tests are included in the ordinary completion map.

\emph{Replicas and the ordinary proposal.} Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-boxes}
 S_0=[0,15]^2,\qquad S_1=160e_1+S_0,\qquad S_2=160e_2+S_0,\qquad K=256,\quad h=512,
\end{equation}
where \([0,15]\) is an interval of integers, so each box holds \(K\) replicas. A replica uses both logical inputs at its own transverse displacement. The ordinary proposal at \((i,P,t)\) is the unique letter proposed by at least \(h\) of the \(3K\) replicas in \(S_0\cup S_1\cup S_2\); if there is none, it is \(\bot\). Since \(2h>3K\), two different letters cannot both receive \(h\) votes.

\emph{Two coordinates and the rate tests.} The second chart coordinate records the ordinary proposal. The first, retained coordinate equals the ordinary proposal unless additional sampling-register coordinates, which test the scale of the noise, replace it by \(\bot\). A \emph{ride} is a sampling-register copying interval along a chart row. The tests compare short rides with recent rides, at the scales
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-scales}
 \ell_r=2\cdot4^r-1,\qquad W=\lfloor C_h/\mu\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Here \(\mu\) is the aggregate refresh rate and \(C_h\) is a fixed rational constant selected later. The physical rule reads neither number; \(W\) is used only to analyse the experiments. A fixed bank of \(B\) sampling lanes supplies the test votes. Each binary-coded sampling register has one low code, one unknown code and 126 high codes.

We use the chart and machine construction of \mbox{\cite[Proposition~4.3]{DMNoise2026}} and Lemmas~4.1\nobreakdash--4.2 and~11.2\nobreakdash--11.3 of that preprint, its
finite-history boundary-path estimate
\mbox{\cite[Lemma~11.6]{DMNoise2026}}, its intrinsic experiment and source bounds
\cite[Lemmas~6.1--6.3 and~10.4]{DMNoise2026}, and the scaffold parent and
soundness lemmas \cite[Lemmas~A.3 and~A.2]{DMPhase2026}. We use the replica-majority construction of \cite[Theorem~2.1]{DMFixed2026}, where replicas are called providers, in the tradition of G\'acs--Reif and Toom \cite{GacsReif1988,Toom1980}. The sampling registers are the helpers of \cite{DMNoise2026}. A computation board
is a finite grid carrying successive configurations of the fixed machine;
legality means its local transition and boundary constraints hold. Its
row flag is a binary chart label. A critical gate is the sampling-register test at
the bottom end of a row at one of the two depths specified below.

The rate selects a cutoff depth by separating short rows from long rows. Along short rows few lanes have refreshed, and along recent segments of long rows enough have refreshed for the test to distinguish them. The chart marks which side of this distinction its rows should satisfy. A \emph{cover} decorates its projected geometry with finite-state letters so that legality can be tested locally. The cutoff and the guarded tests at the ends of the critical rows tie this legal geometry to the machine input.

\begin{proposition}[Classical chart interface]\label{c:selector-import}
There is an effective finite chart alphabet and a fixed finite sampling-register rule
with the following properties. For a universal machine \(U\), choose
\(a=n+n_0+1\), with \(n_0\) the fixed offset of
Section~\ref{s:parameters}, item~4, and the nominal window for the
aggregate refresh rate \(\mu\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-window}
 \mathcal J_n=\left(\frac{2C_h}{\ell_a},\frac{21C_h}{10\ell_a}\right).
\end{equation}
For every \(\mu\in\mathcal J_n\), \(W=\lfloor C_h/\mu\rfloor\)
satisfies \(\ell_{a-1}<W\), \(2W\le\ell_a\), and \(W\le4^a-1\). If \(U(n)\) does not halt, the chart has a canonical
projected geometry with cutoff \(a\) with a legal finite-state cover and legal
finite computation boards. If \(U(n)\) halts, every full chart has a wrong
critical gate of one of two types: a short depth-\((a-1)\) gate with flag
zero, or a long depth-\(a\) gate with flag one.

The canonical projected geometry is computable on signed coordinates.
Its sampling-register copy characteristic preserves the base chart row of each lane.
For a fixed candidate critical gate and a deterministic finite footprint,
the intrinsic experiment estimates, namely the bounds \(u_B,z_B,w_B\) of
\eqref{eq:c-eta}, and the finite-history boundary-path estimates
\cite[Lemma~11.6]{DMNoise2026} hold with constants uniform on \(\mathcal J_n\). On halting, the finite
motif radius needed for these estimates is found by a terminating search
through finite locally compatible patches.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Termination of the motif search.} The finite motif search rejects a patch when it contains either of
the two wrong-gate rides. If arbitrarily large survivors remained,
compactness would give a full chart with neither ride, contrary to the
machine construction. This explains both termination on halting and why
the search is not a halting decision procedure.

\emph{The chart cover.} The effective chart cover
uses the northeast (NE) presentation of Le Gloannec and Ollinger
\cite[Theorem~3]{LGO2012}. The limit set of a \(2\times2\) substitution has
an effective finite cover whose full west and south neighbours determine
at most one tile. Their Theorem~4 gives the stronger four-way presentation. The limit set here consists of full-plane colourings
admitting arbitrarily deep compatible desubstitutions. The canonical
projected geometry belongs to it. One can see this by using its finite
output automaton as a state substitution, translating state quadrants
far into the positive quadrant, and taking compatible limits at every
substitution depth. The cover completion and incoming edges fix the
geometry. The flags and the chart bit are fixed by the west and south
inputs, with the horizontal row flag coming from the west and the
vertical flag from the south. The carrier, boundary and interior rules
of \cite[Lemma~11.3 and its preceding update table]{DMNoise2026} set the
computation fields, with the phase read from the geometry letter.

\emph{History slots and rejection.} For west and south history tuples \(w=(w_j)\) and \(s=(s_j)\), slot zero
is the completion of \(w_0,s_0\). The remaining slots copy \(s_{j-1}\)
for \(1\le j<T_{\rm hist}\), as in \cite[Section~11.4]{DMNoise2026}.
Collar overlaps, cutoff compatibility, forbidden halting symbols and
vertical and horizontal history matches only reject the candidate fixed
by the two inputs.

The offset in the machine input makes the computation at the selected depth \(U(n)\).
Let \(V(m)=U(m-1)\), \(m\ge1\). If the scan's first zero is
\(j_*\), it accepts \(j_*+2=2^e\), writes \(e\) tokens and
consumes \(n_0+1\) of them before calling \(V\) on
\(m=e-n_0-1\). At cutoff rank \(a\),
\(j_*=2^{a+1}-2\), so \(m=a-n_0=n+1\) and the simulated
computation is exactly \(U(n)\). The finite scan machine is built
after \(n_0\) is fixed.

\subsection{Total finite initialization}\label{subsec:c-initialization}
A global choice of hidden cover letters need not be computable. Only a finite legal
choice is required by the encoder.

\begin{lemma}[Finite controller initialization]\label{c:initialization}
From \((n,L)\) and a declared finite time horizon one can compute an initial
controller word, without the noise rate or a halting answer.
Suppose that the space-time cone of the stored data through that horizon,
with every initial history slot and all fixed lane and collar offsets,
injects into one chart of the side-\(L\) torus whose seams lie outside it.
For the extended storage horizon used in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{d:storage}, this condition is guaranteed by its size
cutoff \(L_0(n)\). Then on a nonhalting input the word agrees with a
correct-cutoff reference on that entire cone.
Its sampling-register initialization
satisfies the same short upper and recent-long lower experiment bounds as
the classical interface.
\end{lemma}
\par\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Compute and search only the finite space-time region needed for the requested horizon. The legal nonhalting extension supplies a candidate, and a fixed fallback makes the search total.

\begin{proof}
Compute the canonical projection, cutoff and finite payload on a
square containing the
whole cone, every initial history slot and all fixed lane and collar
offsets. Exhaust the finite list of cover-letter assignments on that
square, imposing the computed projection and every local constraint.
Return the first successful assignment, with a fixed fallback if the list
is empty. The algorithm always terminates. On nonhalting, a global cover
of the projected reference supplies a successful finite assignment.
Functionality forces its future on the smaller cone. Assignments for
different sizes need not be restrictions of a common hidden cover.

Initialize sampling registers deterministically: use low on reference flag-one rows
and high on flag-zero rows. Each sampling-register field must use its own base row.
The copy characteristic preserves that row, even when the physical field
index changes its offset. On a short ride, a low initial value can only
remove a contribution until a genuine future mark is seen; hence its
upper bound is preserved. On a long ride, a high initial value dominates
the recent-\(W\) comparison until \(W\) genuine future atoms have become
available. Thereafter the latest genuine mark gives the usual comparison.
This argument also covers an experiment crossing time zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Named sources under layer noise}\label{c:named-sources}
Section~\ref{sec:compilation} compiles the controller so that all microscopic faults at one site in one round have a single source name (Section~\ref{s:source-name}). We record the resulting source field.

\begin{definition}[Raw sources, the marked field and source labels]\label{c:sources-def}
For a site \({\bf z}\) and round \(t\), let \(A_{{\bf z},t}\) record any internal fault at \({\bf z}\) in round \(t\) and let \(E_{{\bf z},t}\) be its independent aggregate refresh (Sections~\ref{s:ws-realization} and~\ref{s:probe}), and put
\[
 H_{{\bf z},t}=A_{{\bf z},t}\lor E_{{\bf z},t},\qquad p=\Pr(H_{{\bf z},t}=1)\le2\mu.
\]
The family \(H\) is independent on physical site-round names. One name can corrupt arbitrary outgoing copies of that site's old word, which may disagree with one another, and its own new output. Accordingly the single name \(({\bf z},t)\) seeds both formal occurrences \(({\bf z},t-1)\) and \(({\bf z},t)\); nonexistent pre-zero sources are omitted. The marked field \(M^0\) is the least finite-start field that contains these seeds and marks a nonfresh vertex whenever marked ordinary parents occur in two distinct replica boxes. For the positive source estimate, use base characteristics and bins of length \(W\); a \emph{source label} is
\[
 \lambda=(i-t,\;P+58t(1,1),\;Q,\;\lfloor t/W\rfloor).
\]
\end{definition}
Source labels do not count independent microscopic faults: an intrinsic test can involve many physical atoms. All occurrences with the same label are kept together, and geometric roles annotate a witness without splitting a label.

A \emph{bin} is an integer block of rounds. A \emph{star} joins appearances of one source, and a \emph{seed} initiates the marked field. A \emph{witness} is a connected derivation or contour for an error. A \emph{certificate} is a finite configuration counted in an error estimate. A group collects appearances with a common label or a common covering ball, as specified in the argument. A \emph{charge} indexes a potential, and a source's \emph{image} projects it into the coordinates used by the code. A catalogue lists local templates by type. Overlay faults occur outside the nominal replacement experiment. In the counting arguments, the cost of a configuration is its probability factor, and its entropy refers to the number of possible descriptions.

\begin{proposition}[Joint source law]\label{c:source-law}
There are fixed finite support lists and computable overlap divisors
\(D_{\rm bin},D_{\rm hist}\), independent of \(W,n\), such that the
following holds for every \(n\) and every aggregate refresh rate
\(\mu\in\mathcal J_n\). Let \(O_\lambda\) mean an internal fault in the complete
hardware support of an experiment in label \(\lambda\), and suppose
\(\Pr(O_\lambda)\le\alpha_{\rm bin}\) for every label \(\lambda\). For any \(g\) distinct source labels,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-gamma}
\begin{gathered}
 \Pr(\textup{all labels have a raw or intrinsic exception})\le\gamma_{\rm src}^g,\\
 \gamma_{\rm src}=\sqrt{2C_h}+\eta_B^{1/6}+\alpha_{\rm bin}^{1/(2D_{\rm bin})},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}\label{eq:c-eta}
 u_B&=2^{-\lfloor3BC_h/100\rfloor},&
 z_B&=2^{-\lfloor BC_h/3\rfloor},&
 w_B&=2^{-\lfloor BC_h^2/50\rfloor},\notag\\
 \eta_B&=\frac{3u_B}{1-4z_B}+2w_B,
 &4z_B&<1.
\end{align}
For \(m\) distinct historical fixed-gate tests the augmented intrinsic
intersection has probability at most \(\theta_{\rm pkt}^m\), where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-theta}
 \theta_{\rm pkt}=\eta_B+\alpha_{\rm bin}^{1/D_{\rm hist}}.
\end{equation}
A larger common divisor can be used for boundary tests.

Every erroneous initialized controller reading has a finite acyclic
backward derivation through ordinary two-box supports, sampling-register corridors,
or these source labels. In characteristic coordinates sampling-register corridors
preserve all five potentials used by the quantum proof. Collapsing them
leaves a finite set of roles and bounded bin shifts.
\end{proposition}
\par\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Bound the statistical tests using their sampling means and tails. Colour the overlapping supports and combine the independent sampling and hardware events to obtain the joint bound.

\begin{proof}
The binary sampling-register coding changes the upper fresh-mark prefactor to
\(127/128\) and the lower to \(126/128\ge 0.98\). On a short depth
\(a-1-r\) ride the mean is less than \(0.53C_h\cdot4^{-r}\); the voting
threshold is at least \(0.75C_h\). On a recent long ride the mean exceeds
\(0.9C_h\), while the threshold is at most \(0.8C_h\). The exponential
moment bounds for these two Bernoulli comparisons give \(u_B,z_B,w_B\);
summing short depths gives the geometric denominator in
\eqref{eq:c-eta}. These are the intrinsic estimates in the classical
interface, with conservative binary-coding prefactors.

Raw \(H\) bins have disjoint physical atoms and probability at most
\(pW\le2C_h\). Baseline intrinsic tests use only \(E\) and fresh baseline mixed ancilla
marks. On \(A=0\) these are the actual marks; dirty outputs on \(A=1\)
can have arbitrary values, so we count them as internal hardware faults. Only
\(E\cap\{A=0\}\) is used to infer an actual uniform product. Distinct characteristics own disjoint lane characteristics, and
every third bin has disjoint intrinsic support; hence \(g\) specified
baseline tests cost at most \(\eta_B^{g/3}\).

An internal exception includes the full support of every short ride and
recent experiment in the bin. Its atoms lie on a fixed characteristic
translate list and in a fixed number of adjacent bins, so there are at
most \(h_0W\) atoms. Its probability is at most \(h_0W\nu\), where
\(\nu=\Pr(A=1)\). Supports can overlap only at differences from a fixed
finite list. A proper colouring of that overlap graph, with at least
one more colour than its degree, yields
\(\Pr(\bigcap O_\lambda)\le\alpha_{\rm bin}^{g/D_{\rm bin}}\).
The \(E\) and \(A\) families are independent. Combine their intrinsic
bounds, then use Cauchy--Schwarz against the raw family, which overlaps
both. Expanding the possible source types gives exactly
\eqref{eq:c-gamma}. Different \(Q\) copies within one selector have disjoint physical
innovations; their copy names remain part of the label.

For historical tests, write an atom of a ride starting at chart endpoint
\(g=(g_x,g_y)\), time \(b\) and replica \(P_b\) as
\[
 x=g_x-\ell+\delta_x,\quad y=g_y+j+\delta_y,
 \quad P=P_b+58\ell(1,1)+\delta_P,
 \quad s=b-\ell+\delta_t.
\]
The offset \(\delta\) ranges over a fixed support list. Equality of a
physical atom between two tests implies
\[
 b-b'=(\delta_x-\delta_t)-(\delta'_x-\delta'_t).
\]
Thus the difference of their start times lies in a fixed finite list,
independently of \(W\) and of the replica path. Colouring gives
\(D_{\rm hist}\), and independence of the baseline and internal
families gives \eqref{eq:c-theta}. This remains a fixed-support statement
when a later union bound chooses paths after seeing the noise.

A historical fixed-gate test is one physical sampling experiment,
given by a gate endpoint \(g\), start time \(b\) and transverse base
\(P_b\). Different annotations or roles of one experiment are the
same test. Its baseline atoms are the refresh coins of its own \(B\)
lanes on its own ride, the atoms with \(\delta=0\) above. On one
gate such an atom determines \(\ell=g_x-x\), \(b=s+\ell\) and
\(P_b=P-58\ell(1,1)\). Across gates, ruler and lane ownership
\cite[Lemmas~10.4 and~11.4]{DMNoise2026} assigns it one owner.
Hence distinct tests, including tests with \(b=b'\), have disjoint
baseline atoms. In Proposition~\ref{c:late-live} they are the
experiments of one candidate gate at distinct transverse bases
\cite[Lemma~11.6]{DMNoise2026}, on distinct copy characteristics.
So the baseline family contributes \(\eta_B^m\) with divisor one,
as in Section~\ref{v:parameters}. Only internal-fault envelopes
overlap. Equality of one envelope atom forces \(b-b'\) and
\(P_b-P'_b\) into fixed finite lists, read off the \(x\)-, \(s\)-
and \(P\)-coordinates above, independent of \(B,W,n\). This bounds
the overlap degree and gives \(D_{\rm hist}\).

Finally, a wrong clean ordinary output has more than \(K\) failing
replicas, so wrong inputs occupy two boxes. A sampling-register error either has
an intrinsic exception or follows its unique copy characteristic to a
wrong driver. That corridor preserves its characteristic potentials;
a driver step then makes positive progress. Expanding the fixed-depth
query and ordinary rule strictly decreases the formal history order,
ending at a named source or the ideal initial reference. This proves
finiteness and acyclicity. The projected role list is independent of the
lane length because its fixed offsets cancel along a complete corridor.
\end{proof}

\subsection{All-start halting estimates}
The estimates so far assume the intended initialization. We now work in one selector marginal and define events that dominate the corresponding events for all initial words at once.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-age coherence]\label{c:coherence}
Let \(\mathrm{MP}({\bf z},t)\) mean that an ordinary parent of \(({\bf z},t)\)
belongs to \(M^0\), and let \(D_s({\bf z},t)\) be the event that a backward
ordinary path of length \(s\) has at least \(\delta s\) such positions,
where \(\delta=1/1404\). Outside the corresponding two \(D_s\) events,
two unmarked live readings at the same logical position and round \(t\),
at replica distance \(d\), with \(t\ge s\ge\max(1,2d)\), agree. A clean
child check and this equality certify local west/south compatibility.
There are a computable source-rate cap and constants \(A,a>0\),
independent of \(s\), \(({\bf z},t)\), \(L\) and \(n\), such that below
the cap \(\Pr(D_s({\bf z},t))\le A\exp(-as)\) for every \(s\ge1\)
whenever the depth-\(s\) backward cone of \(({\bf z},t)\) unwraps
injectively. The age of a reading is its round index \(t\).
\end{lemma}
\par\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Pair clean predecessor readings to compare the two proposals. A discrepancy needs many marked predecessors, whose complete contours can be counted by disjoint source slabs.

\begin{proof}
The recurring constants in this proof are derived in Appendix~\ref{q:constants-origin}.
At an unmarked root at most one replica box contains marked ordinary
inputs. Therefore at least \(h-K=256\) successful replicas read actual
unmarked words. When neither comparison root has \(\mathrm{MP}\), pair
replicas by a unit shift reducing their signed \(\ell^1\) distance.
There are \(3\cdot16\cdot15=720\) pairs. At most
\(2(3K-h)=512\) fail one of the two checks, leaving a reducing pair.
Functionality selects a common conflicting west or south input. At an
exceptional step choose any clean successful replica at each root;
functionality still selects a conflicting logical input, and replica
distance increases by at most 350. After one step both readings are
actual first-coordinate words. Distance-zero conflict is then impossible,
including at time zero. With fewer than \(2\delta s\) exceptional
steps the final distance is strictly less than
\[
 d-s+351(2\delta s)=d-s/2,
\]
a contradiction. For compatibility use one clean child replica, and
compare its two actual old inputs with the chosen reference inputs.

For the probability bound, use potentials
\(3(\chi_1+\chi_2),-3\chi_1,-3\chi_2\), where
\(\chi=P+58t(1,1)\). Good gains are 129, the largest loss is 351,
and a grouped old/new name has total oscillation 696. The balanced
terminal-arm count of Lemma~\ref{q:frontier} gives fewer than \(17f\) positive edges for \(f\)
distinct names; the conservative budget \(64f\) also covers the group
stars. Attach complete contours at the marked parents along the selected
path. Retain each complete contour and restart at least two times before
its earliest physical name. Disjoint source slabs then cover at most
\(68F\) path positions with \(F\) distinct names. Thus
\(F\ge\lceil\delta s/68\rceil\), and the path and contours form a
connected graph of \(O(F/\delta)\) edges. A fixed finite-degree graph has
at most \(K_\delta^F\) such rooted annotated graphs. Summing their
probabilities \(p^F\) proves the estimate at a small enough fixed rate.
\end{proof}

The constants \(A_n,a_n\) in Proposition~\ref{c:late-live} are decay
constants local to this section; the window endpoints \(a_n,b_n\) of
Section~\ref{s:parameters} are different.

\begin{proposition}[Late unmarked liveness]\label{c:late-live}
If \(U(n)\) halts, there are finite \(A_n,a_n>0\), uniform for
\(\mu\in \mathcal J_n\) and independent of \(L\), \({\bf z}\) and \(t\),
such that whenever the depth-\(t\) determining cone of \(({\bf z},t)\)
unwraps injectively on the physical torus,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-late}
 \Pr(\textup{some initial word gives an unmarked live reading at }({\bf z},t))
 \le A_n(1+t)^b e^{-a_nt}.
\end{equation}
The same conclusion applies to ordinary-proposal readings. Here the
exponent \(b\) is fixed by the finite cone geometry.
\end{proposition}
\par\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Use finite-age coherence to expose a wrong critical test in a late valid chart. Count the complete boundary explanations that could prevent its error from reaching the target.

\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{c:coherence} at the deterministic roots of a late box.
The finite-history construction in the classical interface chooses that
box so its minimum age dominates twice its replica diameter. Its
exception has a polynomial-times-exponential bound. On the complement,
a live unmarked target determines a coherent same-history reference
patch. The halting motif supplies a wrong critical gate \(\Gamma\) at
time \(b_\Gamma\), with
\(t-b_\Gamma\ge c_0t\,-\,O_n(1)\) for a fixed \(c_0>0\) given by the
halting motif. Candidate gate positions, types, finite
phase residues and footprints range over a deterministic polynomial
list. The reference labels of a selected history are not included in
that list.

For each candidate let \(F_\Gamma\) be its deterministic slab footprint:
the routed vertices \((i_\Gamma,P,b_\Gamma)\), \((i_\Gamma,P,b_\Gamma+1)\)
and \((i_\Gamma+1,P,b_\Gamma+1)\), with \(i_\Gamma\) the candidate's first northeast
position (as below) and \(P\) in the candidate's
transverse footprint. Let \(S_\Gamma\subset F_\Gamma\) be the primitive
boundary exception set: every vertex of \(F_\Gamma\) carrying a selected
complete raw or deep certificate whose flags have physical time at most
\(b_\Gamma+1\), or a failed intrinsic test, enlarged by the packed balls
of \cite[Lemma~11.6]{DMNoise2026}, taken in characteristic distance and
intersected with \(F_\Gamma\), so that each ball covers both slab rows.
A flag of a boundary name at one of its formal occurrences in
\(F_\Gamma\) is a complete raw certificate there, with \(f=1\). Let
\(R_+\) contain both formal occurrences of every flagged raw physical
name of time at least \(b_\Gamma+2\).
Define \(M_\Gamma\) as the least field containing \(S_\Gamma\cup R_+\)
and closed under the two-box ordinary-parent rule at every child time
\(s\ge b_\Gamma+1\), including the upper row of the slab.
There are no other births. Deep queries, sampling
registers and the censor only replace a live proposal by \(\bot\)
(Section~\ref{c:chart}), so none of them is a birth. These definitions use only noise and the
candidate geometry. Minimize flag count and then a finite lexicographic
code to select certificates. The ordinary entropy count already includes
all these types. The envelope can overinclude actual errors, which only
strengthens domination of the target.

Reserve all boundary raw physical names through \(b_\Gamma+1\). The
formal slab is \(\{b_\Gamma,b_\Gamma+1\}\), and includes new seeds at
both first northeast positions \(i_\Gamma\) and \(i_\Gamma+1\).
Future raw names start at \(b_\Gamma+2\). Use west when
\(x>i_\Gamma\), and south on \(x=i_\Gamma\); this stays in the
northeast cone. Full supports, rather than their realized flagged subsets,
determine all restart cuts. These conventions separate boundary and
future raw names exactly.

The finite-history boundary-path estimate of the classical interface
extends to these grouped sources as follows. A genuine wrong live deep
query traverses all \(T_{\rm hist}=1024\) levels, so its \(64f\) budget requires
at least sixteen distinct names. A faulty current lookup is a direct
local exception instead. Pack complete raw or deep witnesses in disjoint
physical source slabs. Historical intrinsic tests obey
\eqref{eq:c-theta}; Hölder gives raw weight \((2\mu)^{3f/4}\) and
intrinsic weight \(\theta_{\rm pkt}^{m/4}\). The lag, gap and endpoint choices
cost at most \(W^{6f/16}\). Since \(W\le C_h/\mu\), the resulting raw
power per name is \(\mu^{3/8}\). Expand any propagated upper-row boundary terminal through this one
ordinary level to its primitive seeds, adding only fixed-range support
edges and fixed roles, with no new intrinsic experiment or lag choice.
Keep the expanded boundary contour whole, including old/new raw
occurrences, before making restart cuts. The boundary and future
physical-name sets remain disjoint by the preceding time partition.
Enlarge the fixed contour and entropy constants for this level before
choosing the rate cap. Consequently the strengthened boundary field
satisfies
\[
 \Pr(\text{a backward }N\text{-path at times }s\ge b_\Gamma+1\text{ has at least}
              \ N/2\text{ vertices in }M_\Gamma)
 \le A_\partial e^{-a_\partial N}.
\]
Choosing \(B\) makes the intrinsic base small, padding makes its
internal overlay small, and the nominal rate bound makes the remaining
raw base small. These choices are uniform on each selected window.

Boundary domination places the target in \(M_\Gamma\).
On the gate row let a routed footprint vertex be read live by a routed
child on row \(b_\Gamma+1\). If the copy read is flagged, its name has
physical time \(b_\Gamma+1\) and is a one-flag certificate at that
gate-row vertex, even when its actual output is \(\bot\). Otherwise its
actual output is live, and the intrinsic/raw/deep alternative (item~3
of the halting proof in \cite[Section~11.7]{DMNoise2026}) applies.
Either way the vertex is in \(S_\Gamma\). On the next row, a routed
child whose own output is flagged carries a one-flag certificate by its
boundary name of time \(b_\Gamma+1\), and so lies in \(S_\Gamma\).
Otherwise a live ordinary completion has \(h>K\) successful replicas
with live selected-index inputs on the gate row, by bottom-spreading.
These inputs are in \(S_\Gamma\) and occupy two boxes, so closure at
\(s=b_\Gamma+1\) marks the child.
The same induction continues at every later row and includes ordinary
proposals before censoring
\cite[Lemma~11.6, domination paragraph]{DMNoise2026}.
Put \(U_\Gamma=M_\Gamma\setminus M^0\) at times
\(s\ge b_\Gamma+1\). A vertex of \(U_\Gamma\) is not a future raw
seed, since that name also seeds \(M^0\). Strictly above the slab,
membership therefore comes from a closure step with selected marked
parents in two boxes. If all those selected parents belonged to
\(M^0\), its own two-box closure would mark the child.
Hence a selected parent belongs to \(U_\Gamma\). Following such a
parent strictly decreases time until it reaches the slab.
The result is an actual Boolean path of length at least
\(t-b_\Gamma-1\), entirely in \(M_\Gamma\) at times
\(s\ge b_\Gamma+1\). Apply the
boundary-path estimate, union over the candidate list, and add the
coherence exception. This proves \eqref{eq:c-late} simultaneously over
initial words.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rare live proposals]\label{c:rare}
Fix \((i,Q)\) and \(t_0\ge0\). With \(\Theta_h\) denoting the
replica threshold (for a transverse field \(V\), \(\Theta_h(V)(P)=1\)
when \(V(P+\delta)=1\) for at least \(h\) of the \(3K\) offsets
\(\delta\in S_0\cup S_1\cup S_2\)) and \(B^{\rm fr}_s=H_s\lor H_{s+1}\) at \((i,P,Q)\), put
\[
 \mathcal W_{t_0-1}\equiv0,\qquad
 \mathcal W_s=B^{\rm fr}_s\lor\Theta_h(\mathcal W_{s-1}),\qquad
 N_s=\Theta_h(\mathcal W_s)\quad(s\ge t_0).
\]
For every initial word and every \(t>t_0\), if the ordinary proposal
at \((i,P,Q,t)\) is live, then (a) a direct destination exception
occurs: \(H\) at \((i,P,Q)\) at one of the times \(t-1\), \(t\), \(t+1\), or (b) \(N_{t-1}(P)=1\), or (c) there is a
backward path of live readings without \(B^{\rm fr}\) from \((i,P,Q,t)\)
to time \(t_0\), through south inputs of successful replicas.
Every new \(N\)-birth either has old support in two boxes or uses
at least 256 distinct fresh replica positions. Tile the transverse
plane by cubes \(C_c=176c+[0,176)^2\), \(c\in\Z^2\). For
\(P\in C_c\),
\(P+(S_0\cup S_1\cup S_2)\subset D_c=176c+[0,352)^2\).
The seed group \(\Sigma_{c,s}\), meaning that some \(P\in C_c\)
has a fresh alternative at time \(s\), depends only on \(H\) on
\(D_c\times\{s,s+1\}\) and has probability at most
\(N_{\rm seed}p^{256}\), where
\mbox{\(N_{\rm seed}=\tbinom{2\cdot352^2}{256}\)}.
Within each of the eight classes \((c\bmod2,s\bmod2)\) these supports
are pairwise disjoint. Thus \(g\) distinct specified seed groups
have joint probability at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-rare}
 (N_{\rm seed}p^{256})^{g/8}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\par\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Follow live south inputs backward until a fresh source or the initial row is reached, then count fresh source groups on disjoint doubled cubes.
\begin{proof}
A live completion needs both inputs, so selecting south keeps
\(i,Q\) fixed. If (a) and (c) fail, every backward chain of live
readings through successful replicas ends at a vertex with \(B^{\rm fr}\)
at time at least \(t_0\). A non-\(B^{\rm fr}\) live reading equals its live
ordinary proposal and has \(h\) successful replicas with live south
inputs. Induction up these finite chains puts every chain vertex
in \(\mathcal W\) and the root in \(N_{t-1}\), proving (b).
If fewer than \(h-K=256\) replicas are fresh, more than \(K\)
old replicas remain and occupy two boxes. Otherwise there are 256
distinct spatial positions, hence 256 distinct physical names, even
though either of two times can supply each position. The replica
boxes lie in \([0,175]^2\), which proves the displayed doubled-cube
inclusion. A union over subsets of 256 of the \(2\cdot352^2\)
physical names gives the seed bound. The four spatial cube parities
and two time parities give eight disjoint-support classes. One class
contains at least \(g/8\) of any specified collection, proving
\eqref{eq:c-rare} by independence of \(H\).

On a halting window take \(t_0=\lfloor t/2\rfloor\). Alternative
(c) gives either an unmarked live reading of age at least \(t_0\)
in the depth-\(t\) cone, excluded by Proposition~\ref{c:late-live}
with a polynomial union, or an all-\(M^0\) backward path of length
\(\lceil t/2\rceil\), excluded by the raw-path tail. These are the
events unioned in Propositions~\ref{c:path-tail} and~\ref{f:ancestor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Classical control of arbitrary data paths]\label{c:path-tail}
For each fixed finite catalogue of quantum dependency templates, the
rate bound can be chosen so that, on a halting window, the probability of
an \(s\)-step permitted data path through a specified physical initial
site is at most \(A'_n(1+s)^{b'}e^{-a'_ns}\), where \(A'_n,a'_n>0\)
depend only on \(n\) (uniformly over \(\eps\in I_n\), the size \(L\), the
site and \(s\)) and \(b'\) only on the catalogue. For the folded appendix, a path may switch among its four sheets
arbitrarily. It must cross at each step either its assigned live
selector or a named local fault at the conditional-reset block. The bound is
simultaneous over all initial combined classical words and holds in
finite cones that unwrap injectively on the physical torus.
\end{proposition}
\par\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Combine the late valid-reading bound with the raw path bound in the data cone. Any residual quantum dependence must repeatedly pay for an exceptional classical reading or a hardware fault.

\begin{proof}
Union Proposition~\ref{c:late-live} and the marginal raw-path tails
over the selector collection and the polynomially many roots in the late actual
physical data cone. This union uses no independence between selectors.
For a late reading at time \(t\), take \(t_0=\lfloor t/2\rfloor\)
in Lemma~\ref{c:rare}. The union excludes alternative~(c), leaving
rare-field ancestry or a direct destination exception. Attach complete rare contours in
their own orientations and direct exceptions to the physical
path. Keep entire deterministic doubled-time cube supports and restart
below their earliest full physical time minus two. The union of the
finite controller collection and all possible programmed quantum edges is
a finite-degree graph, irrespective of the initial profile.

Let \(g\) count distinct typed rare groups and \(k_{\rm dir}\) distinct direct
physical names. With \(n_{\rm sel}=1\) in three dimensions and
\(n_{\rm sel}=4\) in the folded appendix, marginal bound \eqref{eq:c-rare} and
Hölder across selector families give seed intersection weight at most
\((N_{\rm seed}p^{256})^{g/(8n_{\rm sel})}\). Direct tests have weight
\(p^{k_{\rm dir}}\); they can overlap seed supports. Cauchy--Schwarz between
these two intersections therefore gives the combined bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-mixed}
 (N_{\rm seed}p^{256})^{g/(16n_{\rm sel})}p^{k_{\rm dir}/2}.
\end{equation}
The rare power is \(256/(16n_{\rm sel})\ge4>0\). Temporal coverage and connected
edge budgets are fixed maxima over the four orientations. A length-\(s\)
path requires \(g+k_{\rm dir}\ge s/B_{\rm rare}\), while its annotated graph
count is at most \(K_{\rm rare}^{g+k_{\rm dir}}\). Choose the architecture's
cap so both entropy-weighted bases in \eqref{eq:c-mixed} are at most
\(1/64\). The resulting geometric sum decays exponentially. Polynomial
unions over accessible final roots and the earlier finite-cone
exceptions preserve that decay for the full joint physical history.
\end{proof}
